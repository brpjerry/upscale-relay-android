Seeking is roughly ten times faster.

**A far seek now shows a frame in about a second.** It used to take 13–20
seconds, and the wait grew the further into the video you jumped — seeking to
5 minutes cost less than seeking to 20, regardless of how far you had moved.
The server was never the slow part: it had the first upscaled frame ready
within a second of the request.

The delay was in how this app handed the original file to mpv for audio and
subtitles. mpv places an external audio or subtitle track at the current
playback position at the moment the track is chosen — and while a video is
still loading, that position is zero. The relay stream, meanwhile, starts at
the point you seeked to. So the audio track opened at the beginning of the
episode and mpv worked its way forward to your seek position by decoding
everything in between, which is exactly why the wait scaled with how far into
the episode you landed. The picture had been decoded and was sitting there the
whole time, waiting for audio to catch up.

The original file is now attached after playback has started, when the
position is known, so the audio and subtitle tracks jump straight to it.

Measured on a Galaxy Tab S9 Ultra against a 23-minute episode:

| Seek target | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 5:05 (before) | 2.9 s | 12.8 s |
| 11:18 (before) | 3.3 s | 19.6 s |
| anywhere (now) | 1.0 s | 1.5 s |

Audio/video drift stays in the microseconds and the decoder drops no frames,
including through a rapid eight-seek storm. A subtitle track or a non-default
audio track you have chosen now also survives the seeks that follow it, which
it did not always do before.

This release is signed with the same key as v0.15.0 and v0.16.0 and upgrades
over them normally. Moving from a locally built debug APK still requires an
uninstall, because debug and release signatures differ — export your data
first.
